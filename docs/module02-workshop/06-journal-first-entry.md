## Concept and Decision

**Current concept and intended experience:**

Rapid Defense is a two-player physical card game where one player acts as the attacker and the other acts as the defender. The attacker creates and sends threats toward a protected area, while the defender identifies incoming threats, decides which threat is most urgent, and uses the appropriate response to stop it. The intended experience is fast-paced and pressured, with the defender having to make quick decisions when multiple threats are present.

**Design question or decision:**

The main design question is whether having multiple threats present at the same time creates meaningful choices for the defender. I want to determine whether the defender is actually prioritizing threats and making decisions about what to handle first, rather than simply matching each threat with its corresponding response.

**Selected concept and alternatives kept:**

I selected Rapid Defense over Speed Sorting and Rule Change. Rapid Defense has a clear recurring decision loop: identify threats, prioritize them, and choose the correct response. It also provides direct interaction between two players because the attacker actively creates problems for the defender. Speed Sorting and Rule Change will be kept as alternative concepts in case Rapid Defense does not produce interesting decisions during prototyping.

**Peer feedback that affected the decision, if received (otherwise say unavailable):**

I did not receive much direct feedback from my group because I had to leave the workshop. My decision was primarily based on comparing the three concepts and identifying which one had the clearest interaction, recurring decision, and feasible physical prototype.

## Reading Connection

Choose one idea from Chapter 1: Having the Idea, The Treatment, or Feasibility.

**Section and specific idea:**

**Convergence — critically examining the concept and anticipating flaws before continuing development.** Chapter 1 explains that after developing an idea, the designer needs to use critical judgment to determine whether the different parts of the concept will actually work together. It also recommends anticipating flaws so they can be corrected before development becomes more expensive.

**Relevance today:** Still relevant

**Reason and supporting example:**

This idea is relevant because Rapid Defense currently has a clear concept, but I do not yet know whether its main mechanic will actually create interesting gameplay. The game is built around multiple threats competing for the defender's attention, but simply having several threats on the table does not guarantee that the defender will have meaningful choices. The concept therefore needs to be tested critically instead of assuming that the intended decision-making will work as planned.

**Connection to a choice, question or proposed test for my game:**

The convergence idea directly connects to my decision to make the first prototype focus on the game's central risk. I will test whether multiple simultaneous threats force the defender to make meaningful prioritization decisions. If the defender can simply identify the matching response for each threat without having to choose between them, then the current design will need to change before additional mechanics are added.

## Progress and Evidence

**What I created, changed, tested, or decided:**

I compared Speed Sorting, Rapid Defense, and Rule Change and selected Rapid Defense as the concept to explore further. I defined the basic attacker-versus-defender gameplay loop and established the boundary for the first physical prototype. I also identified meaningful defender prioritization as the main risk that needs to be tested.

**Direct artifact link or specific observation:**

The Workshop Idea Bank records the comparison and selection of Rapid Defense. It identifies the recurring decision as identifying threats, prioritizing them, and choosing the correct response. It also identifies the main risk as whether the defender actually has meaningful choices about which threat to respond to first.

**What the evidence confirms:**

The concept has a clear recurring decision and can be tested with a small physical prototype. The attacker actively creates problems while the defender must determine which problems to handle first. The concept can therefore be reduced to a basic threat-and-response interaction for the first test.

**What remains uncertain:**

It remains uncertain whether the defender will have meaningful choices when multiple threats appear. There is a risk that the game could become a simple reaction and matching exercise where the defender always handles the most obvious threat rather than making a meaningful prioritization decision.

## Next Action

**Next prototype, reader test, or design decision:**

Create the smallest physical prototype using a small deck of simple threat cards, a small deck of response cards, and a card or token representing the protected area. Test several rounds with multiple threats appearing at the same time.

**Uncertainty it will address:**

The test will determine whether the defender has meaningful choices about which threat to respond to first, rather than simply matching the available response to the first threat they notice. This will test the main design risk identified during concept selection and provide evidence for whether the core Rapid Defense loop should be developed further.
