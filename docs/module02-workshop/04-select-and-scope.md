# IT265 Module 2: Select and Scope a Concept
| Concept | Clear recurring decision? | Strongest appeal | Feasible first physical prototype? | Risk to test |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Speed Sorting | Yes — identify the rule, decide where the card belongs, and sort it. | Fast reactions and adapting to changing rules. | Yes — can be tested with index cards and a few sorting rules. | The changing rules could become confusing or make the game feel repetitive. |
| Rapid Defense | Yes — identify threats, prioritize them, and choose the correct response. | Direct two-player interaction where one player creates problems and the other reacts. | Yes — can be tested with small decks of threat and response cards. | The attacker and defender need to have meaningful choices rather than one player simply reacting automatically. |
| Rule Change | Yes — identify the current rule, find the correct card, and adapt when the rule changes. | Constantly changing conditions create pressure and require players to reassess their strategy. | Yes — only a few rule cards are needed for a first test. | Too many rules could make the game confusing or difficult to balance. |

Selected concept and reason:

> Rapid Defense. The two-player attacker-versus-defender structure gives the concept a clear source of interaction and pressure. It also has a straightforward recurring decision that can be tested with a small physical prototype. The prototype can focus on whether choosing which threat to respond to creates interesting decisions.

Feedback that changed or confirmed my choice:

> I did not receive much direct feedback from my group because I had to leave the workshop. Based on my own comparison of the three concepts, the clear two-player interaction and prioritization mechanic make Rapid Defense the concept I want to explore further.

Other ideas to keep for later:

> Speed Sorting and Rule Change. Both have clear gameplay loops and could be revisited if Rapid Defense does not produce interesting decisions during prototyping.

First physical prototype boundary:

> The first prototype will only include two players, a small deck of threat cards, a small deck of response cards, and a protected area for the defender. The prototype will focus on the basic attacker-versus-defender loop rather than creating a complete game.

One feature to defer:

> Increasing difficulty through increasingly complex or numerous threats. The first prototype will use a small number of simple threat types.

One risk to test next:

> Whether the defender actually has meaningful choices about which threat to respond to first, rather than simply playing the matching response card as quickly as possible.
