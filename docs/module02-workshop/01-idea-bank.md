# IT265 Module 2: Idea Bank
1. Inspiration: Spit/Speed (card game)  
   1. Players have to quickly recognize possible moves and react before their opponent  
   2. Explore how speed, pressure, and quick choices could work in a different type of game  
2. Brainstorm game Concepts:  
   1. Speed sorting  
      1. Players are given a hand of cards with different colors, numbers, symbols, or categories  
      2. Players must quickly sort their cards into the correct piles based on a current sorting rule  
      3. The sorting rule can change during the game, forcing players to pay attention and adapt  
      4. Players race against each other to correctly sort their cards before their opponent  
      5. Players could have different ways to score depending on how quickly or accurately they sort  
      6. Main idea: quickly recognizing where a card belongs while adapting to changing rules  
   2. Rapid defense  
      1. Players are trying to protect a central object, location, or character from incoming threats  
      2. Threat cards are played into different areas around the player, creating multiple problems at once  
      3. Each threat requires a specific type of response card to defeat it  
      4. Players must quickly identify which threat they can respond to and decide which threat is the most urgent  
      5. More threats can be introduced as the game continues, increasing the pressure on the player  
      6. In a two-person version, players could compete to survive the longest or send threats toward each other  
      7. Main idea: quickly identifying threats and deciding what to deal with first  
   3. Competitive matching  
      1. Two players have their own hands or sets of cards containing different colors, numbers, symbols, or combinations  
      2. A shared card or set of cards determine what players are currently trying to match  
      3. Players race to find a matching card and play it before their opponent  
      4. Some cards could have multiple possible matches, forcing players to decide whether to play immediately or wait  
      5. Special cards could allow players to block an opponent, steal a match, or change what counts as a match  
      6. Players continue matching until one player runs out of cards or reaches a certain score  
      7. Main idea: quickly recognizing opportunities while deciding when and how to act  
   4. Rule change  
      1. Players have a shared collection of cards with different characteristics such as colors, numbers, and symbols  
      2. A rule card determines what players are currently supposed to look for or play  
      3. Players race to identify the correct card according to the current rule  
      4. The rule changes periodically, requiring players to quickly stop following the old rule and adjust to the new one  
      5. Players could earn points for being the first to correctly respond to each rule  
      6. Some rule changes could be announced while players are already making their move, increasing the pressure  
      7. Main idea: quickly adapting when the conditions of the game change  
   5. Speed puzzle  
      1. Players receive cards that can be arranged into patterns, sequences, or structures  
      2. Players have a limited amount of time to complete the current puzzle  
      3. New cards or obstacles can be introduced while players are solving the puzzle  
      4. Players must decide which part of the puzzle to work on first instead of trying to solve everything at once  
      5. Players could compete to complete the puzzle first or work together against a time limit  
      6. Different puzzle cards could introduce new challenges or restrictions  
      7. Main idea: combining quick decision-making with problem solving under time pressure  
3. Categorize each idea  
   1. Speed sorting \- Ready to pitch:  
      1. Has a clear repeating action: identity → decide → sort. It could be prototyped with simple cards or objects  
   2. Rapid defense \- Ready to pitch  
      1. Has an obvious recurring decision: determine the threat → choose a response. The difficulty can be increased easily by adding more threats or making them more difficult to respond to  
   3. Competitive matching \- Unclear loop  
      1. The basic matching idea is clear, but it needs another mechanic to make the moment-to-moment decisions more interesting  
   4. Rule change \- Scope risk  
      1. Changing rules could create interesting decisions, but designing and balancing many rules could become complicated  
   5. Speed puzzle \- Save for later  
      1. It has potential, but the concept is broad and needs a more specific puzzle mechanic before it is ready to prototype  
4. Shortlist two or three workshop targets  
   1. Speed sorting  
      1. The core decision is whether a card belongs in one category or another, and the player has to make that decision quickly  
      2. The changing sorting rules could create additional pressure and force players to adapt  
      3. A first prototype could be made quickly using index cards with different colors, numbers, or symbols  
   2. Rapid defense  
      1. The recurring decision is which threat to respond to first and what response to use  
      2. Having multiple threats appear at once could force players to prioritize rather than simply react to the first thing they see  
      3. A basic prototype could use cards or tokens to represent threats and response cards  
   3. Rule change  
      1. The recurring decision is figuring out what action is currently correct and adapting when the rule changes  
      2. This concept most directly explores the feeling of having to quickly reassess the situation  
      3. A simple prototype could use a small number of rule cards and a deck of cards with different characteristics
