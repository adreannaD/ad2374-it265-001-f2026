# One-Page Treatment

**Working game title:** Rapid Defense

Four-Sentence Core

1. **Premise:** Two players take on opposing roles: one player is the attacker, creating threats that move toward a protected area, while the other player is the defender, trying to stop those threats before they get through.  
2. **Experience and loop:** The game should feel fast-paced and pressured, with the defender repeatedly identifying incoming threats, deciding which one is the most urgent, and choosing the appropriate response while the attacker decides which threats to create and when to play them.  
3. **Goal and pressure:** The attacker wins by getting enough threats past the defender, while the defender wins by successfully stopping the threats for a set number of rounds; as play continues, multiple threats can appear at the same time, forcing the defender to prioritize rather than simply respond to one threat at a time.  
4. **Scope:** The distinctive feature of Rapid Defense is the direct interaction between the attacker creating problems and the defender deciding which problems to handle first; the first prototype will use affordable physical materials such as index cards or tokens and will focus only on the basic threat-and-response loop.

Treatment Draft

**Rapid Defense** is a two-player physical card game in which one player acts as an attacker and the other as a defender. The attacker creates threats and sends them toward a protected area, while the defender must prevent those threats from getting through. The game is designed around the feeling of being under pressure and having to make quick decisions rather than having unlimited time to consider every possible move.

The defender's main recurring decision is to identify the incoming threats, determine which one is the most urgent, and choose the appropriate response. The attacker creates pressure by deciding which threats to play and when to introduce them, giving both players an active role in the game's progression. When multiple threats are present at once, the defender cannot necessarily respond to everything, creating a decision about what to prioritize. The goal is for the attacker to get enough threats through, to win, while the defender tries to stop the threats for a predetermined number of rounds.

The first version will stay intentionally small. A prototype can be made with a small deck of threat cards, a small deck of response cards, and a card or area representing what the defender is protecting. The prototype will focus on whether having multiple threats to choose between creates meaningful decisions for the defender. More complicated threats, additional abilities, and increasing difficulty will be left for later versions. This keeps the first test affordable and focused while allowing the core attacker-versus-defender interaction to be evaluated before expanding the game.
