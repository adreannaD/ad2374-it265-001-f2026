# IT265 Module 2: Concept Pitch Cards
1. Working title: Speed Sorting  
   1. Player role and situation: You are racing against another player to sort a constantly changing set of cards based on their colors, numbers, symbols, or other characteristics.  
   2. Repeated decision or action: Quickly identify the current sorting rule, decide where each card belongs, and place it in the correct pile. The sorting rule can change during the game.  
   3. Goal, pressure, or ending: Be the first player to correctly sort all of your cards. Players are pressured to act quickly while also paying attention to changes in the sorting rule.  
   4. Hook: The sorting rule can suddenly change, forcing players to constantly adapt instead of relying on the same strategy.  
   5. Smallest useful physical prototype: A deck of index cards with different colors, numbers, and symbols, plus 2–3 simple sorting rules and two players.  
   6. Question I want listeners to answer: Does changing the sorting rule make the game more interesting, or does it make the game confusing?  
2. Working title: Rapid Defense  
   1. Player role and situation: Two players compete as an attacker and defender. The attacker sends threat cards toward the defender's protected area, while the defender uses response cards to stop them.  
   2. Repeated decision or action: The attacker chooses which threats to send and when to play them. The defender identifies each threat, chooses the appropriate response, and decides which threat to deal with first.  
   3. Goal, pressure, or ending: The attacker wins by getting enough threats past the defender. The defender wins by successfully stopping the threats for a set number of rounds.  
   4. Hook: The attacker is actively creating problems while the defender has to quickly prioritize which threats to handle first.  
   5. Smallest useful physical prototype: A small deck of threat cards, a small deck of response cards, and one card representing the defender's protected area.  
   6. Question I want listeners to answer: Does having multiple threats to choose between create meaningful decisions for the defender?  
3. Working title: Rule Change  
   1. Player role and situation: You are competing against another player while following a constantly changing rule. A shared rule card determines what type of card players should currently be looking for or playing.  
   2. Repeated decision or action: Identify the current rule, search your cards for the correct response, and play it before your opponent. When the rule changes, quickly adjust to the new rule.  
   3. Goal, pressure, or ending: Earn points by being the first player to correctly respond to each rule. The game ends after a set number of rounds or when a player reaches a target score.  
   4. Hook: Players cannot settle into one strategy because the rules keep changing, forcing them to constantly reassess the situation.  
   5. Smallest useful physical prototype: A small deck of cards with different colors, numbers, and symbols, plus 3–5 simple rule cards and two players.  
   6. Question I want listeners to answer: Do the changing rules create an exciting challenge, or do they make the game too difficult to understand?
